When placed in an electric field, a charge will experience a force, gaining energy relative to the field strength and its charge:









$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=Eq \\
[\mathsf{M}\mathsf{L}\mathsf{T}^{-2}]&=[\mathsf{M}\mathsf{L}\mathsf{T}^{-3}\mathsf{I}^{-1}][\mathsf{I}\mathsf{T}]
\end{aligned}
$$









This energy per unit charge is known as electric potential, and is measured in volts ($V$).









$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac{W}{q} \\
[\mathsf{M}\mathsf{L}^2\mathsf{T}^{-3}\mathsf{I}^{-1}]&=\frac{[\mathsf{M}\mathsf{L}^{2}\mathsf{T}^{-2}]}{[\mathsf{I}\mathsf{T}]}
\end{aligned}
$$









This electric field strength is dependent on potential difference and distance of conductor:









$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\frac{V}{d} \\
[\mathsf{M}\mathsf{L}\mathsf{T}^{-3}\mathsf{I}^{-1}]&=\frac{[\mathsf{M}\mathsf{L}^2\mathsf{T}^{-3}\mathsf{I}^{-1}]}{[\mathsf{L}]}
\end{aligned}
$$
