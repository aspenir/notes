Often just knowing the order of magnitude of a value is enough to know if something is possible.

Fermi questions are seemingly overly specific questions to which an answer must be within an order of magnitude of the answer, and must be deduced with no or very little data, instead estimated using intuition.

e.g. Energy converted in a year by 15% efficiency solar cells covering a typical residential roof, with efficiency $1400Wm^{-2}$
$$
\text{efficiency}=10^{-1} \\
\text{area}=10^2 \\
\text{constant}=10^3 \\
\text{seconds per year }=10^7 \\
\text{efficiency } \times \text{constant} \times \text{area} =10^3 \\
\text{area}\times\text{seconds per year}=10^{10}J
$$

e.g. The kinetic energy of an articulated lorry at motorway speed limit
$$\begin{align}
m=10^4 \\
v=10^1 ms^{-1}\\
E&=\frac{mv^{2}}{2} \\
&=5\times 10^{5} \\
&\approx 10^6
\end{align}$$
e.g. The cost of running a filament lamp all day
$$
I \triangleq 10^{-2}A\\
V \triangleq 10^2V\\
t \triangleq 10^7 s\\
P=10^7 J\\
\frac{£}{P}=10^{-7} £ / J\\
£=1
$$
