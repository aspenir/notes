We ind an average if we _assume_ a value is constant with respect to another variable. For example, when measuring the length of a cuboid, we can measure the length multiple times across its width/height, as its length may vary slightly across its other dimensions.

### Timing errors
Measuring time intervals (especially when humans are involved) is guaranteed to have timing errors. However, this error is random, meaning we can take multiple readings to improve our measurements' accuracy.

1. Easier to synchronise with multiple oscillations than with one
2. If you measure the number of oscillations incorrectly, you can minimise this uncertainty (as "1-off" errors are relatively minimal with a greater "count")
3. Repeats are subject to human rhythm - some are over, some are under - averages help balance these out

>[!warning] This random error is not reaction time!!
