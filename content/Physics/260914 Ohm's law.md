Ohm's law states that for any ohmic conductor at a given temperature, [[260914 Electric potential |potential difference]] and [[260910 Current|current]] are proportional.











$$
\begin{aligned}
V&\propto I \\
\implies R&\text{ is constant}
\end{aligned}
$$











Therefore if $V\not\propto I$, we have a non-ohmic conductor and $R$ is not constant.

### The effect of temperature
##### NTC conductors
Resistance decreases with temperature in semiconductors (such as thermistors) because more electrons gain enough energy to leave their atom and become charge carriers, increasing $n$ in $I=nAve$ (see [[260910 Current |here]]), resulting in a greater current and thus a lower resistance due to $R=\frac{V}{I}$.











$$
\begin{aligned}
R=R_{0}e^{\beta(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{T_{0}})}
\end{aligned}
$$











##### PTC conductors
Meanwhile, in metals, atoms vibrate when provided with more energy, causing electrons to collide more and move sslower, reducing $v$ in $I=nAve$ (see [[260910 Current |here]]), resulting in a lower current and thus a higher resistance due to $R=\frac{V}{I}$.











$$
\begin{aligned}
R=R_{0}e^{\frac{\beta}{(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{T_{0}})}}
\end{aligned}
$$
