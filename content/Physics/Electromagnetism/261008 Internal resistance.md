A source of [[261005 Electromotive force|electromotive force]] has an internal resistance, which affects the [[260910 Current|current]] or [[260914 Electric potential|potential difference]] of its circuit.
$$\begin{align}
I&=\frac{\epsilon}{R+r}  \\

V&=\epsilon-Ir
\end{align}$$
where $r$ is the source's internal resistance and $R$ is the _load_ - the total [[260914 Ohm's law|resistance]] of the rest of the circuit.

This is why a $6V^{}$ source might not generate exactly $6V$. They are measured in a circuit with $R=\infty$.

We _cannot_ measure $\epsilon$ or $r$, so we must calculate them using a system of equations, or using a linear graph of potential difference against current ($V=-rI+\epsilon$).

This is also why ideal voltmeters have $\infty$ resistance and ideal ammeters have $0$ resistance, so that there is no potential difference across the ammeter, and no current draw by a voltmeter.
