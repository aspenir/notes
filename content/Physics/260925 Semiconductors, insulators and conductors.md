Materials can interact with charge in three different manners:
- As an insulator
- As a semiconductor
- As a conductor
### Conductors
Conductors are metallic lattices containing delocalised electrons that can carry charge at any temperature.

Resistivity increases with temperature as atoms in the lattice vibrate more vigorously, causing more collisions between charge carrying electrons and nuclei, causing a lower charge carrier mobility and thus a lesser current and a greater resistance.

### Semiconductors
Semiconductors are materials that contain electrons that are delocalised under certain conditions.

When electrons receive enough energy, they can escape their orbit and become delocalised, creating two different charge carriers - an electron which carries a negative charge, and a "hole" - a positively charged valence shell which moves in the direction of conventional current.

Resistivity decreases with temperature as the effect of this carrier number density increasing is exponential while the effect of lattice vibrations are much weaker.

### Insulators
Insulators have a greater force of electrostatic attraction between their nuclei and valence shells, meaning their electrons cannot escape and create charge carriers. This means insulators have a very high resistance under all circumstances.

Resistivity _does_ technically decrease with temperature for the same reason as in [[260925 Semiconductors, insulators and conductors#Semiconductors|semiconductors]], but to a much lesser, almost negligible extent.
